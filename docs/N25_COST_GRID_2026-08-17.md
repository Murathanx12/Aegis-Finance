# N25 — 10bp was an assumption, and one of our headlines was a fact about it

**2026-08-17, Order 11.** Offline window before the paid night. Runs on the
already-spent selection window; the panel grid is exact arithmetic on stored
numbers, so no slice is re-consumed and no p-value moves.

---

## What was already there

The review's premise was that we treat a flat 10bp as settled and that
turnover is what separates survivors from casualties (Novy-Marx & Velikov).

Half of that was already in place, and saying so matters — a suggestion is not
evidence that the thing was missing. `score()` has always charged
`turnover × rate`, so cost has always been **proportional to measured
turnover**, and `breakeven_bps` is reported per predictor precisely so a reader
who disputes our schedule can ignore it. Momentum's 2.6bp is that column doing
its job.

Two things were genuinely missing. Both were cheap.

## 1. The grid

```
     bp  median net   net > 0  >= +3%/yr   detectable
      0      +1.15%       139         60           11
      2      +0.87%       131         55           10
      5      +0.48%       119         50           10
     10      -0.12%        98         39           12
     20      -1.09%        69         31           21
     30      -1.85%        56         21           32
     50      -3.50%        35         11           54
```

**Read the last column carefully — it is not a survivor count.** `detectable`
is `|net| ≥ MDE`, so it rises at high cost because strategies become detectably
**negative**. At 50bp the 54 are mostly confirmed losers. The survivor columns
are the two in the middle, and both fall monotonically.

**At zero cost the wheel still puts only 60 of 206 predictors over +3%/yr.**
So *"nothing published clears the execution standard"* is not a statement about
our cost schedule — it holds with the schedule switched off entirely. That is
the version of the claim worth keeping, because it survives the assumption
being wrong in either direction.

## 2. A rate that varies with liquidity — and it corrects us

We reported several survivors as *"larger in the illiquid tercile"*. That
comparison charged illiquid names the **megacap rate**, which is the assumption
most obviously wrong in the direction that flatters the illiquid cell. Priced at
liquid 5bp / mid 10bp / illiquid 20bp:

* **Two of the illiquid-concentration verdicts flip** — `OptionVolume1` and
  `dCPVolSpread` are no longer larger where they cannot be traded. Part of what
  we called concentration was the cost model.
* Three survive it (`CPVolSpread`, `SmileSlope`, `Tax`), so the pattern is real
  but smaller than the flat-rate table implied.

### The correction that matters most

**"Exactly one detectable net in the liquid tercile" is a fact about 10bp, not
a fact about the panel.**

```
  liquid bp   detectable & positive   which
          0                       4   CPVolSpread, SmileSlope, MomSeason16YrPlus, ShareIss5Y
          5                       3   SmileSlope, MomSeason16YrPlus, ShareIss5Y
         10                       1   ShareIss5Y
         20                       0   —
```

The count is a function of a rate we assumed, so **the sentence has to carry its
rate from now on.** What does *not* change is the size: every one of them sits
within a few percent of its own MDE. One marginal survivor or three marginal
survivors is the same finding, and at 20bp there are none.

## 3. The dividing line is not quite where the citation puts it

```
  band              n   med turn   med gross   med net@10   med breakeven
  low turnover     69       0.36       0.31%       -0.20%           5.5bp
  mid turnover     68       0.59       1.20%       +0.52%          17.2bp
  high turnover    69       2.44       1.51%       -1.05%           7.0bp
```

Low turnover does **not** win on this panel. Its median net is *worse* than the
middle band's, because the low-turnover names have almost no gross edge to
protect — 0.31% against 1.20%.

**Turnover erodes an edge; it cannot manufacture one.** The costs story is
correct for the high-turnover band, where a 1.51% gross edge is spent down to
−1.05%. For the low-turnover band the problem is the numerator, and no cost
assumption rescues it.

## Provenance (§61), recorded before it can drift

The volatility-managed-portfolio literature — Moreira & Muir, and the critique
line (Cederburg/O'Doherty/Wang/Yan; Barroso & Detzel) — was raised **after** our
rule was declared and measured. It reached us through our own N11 ladder and
N12/N18, and its configuration was declared conventional before it was scored.

So it is **corroboration and spends no calendar**:
`EVIDENCE.provenance = NOT_SELECTED_BY_LITERATURE`, asserted by a test, which
also names what would change it — *if one of their outcomes ever picks a rung
for us* (preferring the market book because they found the managed market
portfolio the encouraging case), that is `hypothesis_source` and it caps the
claim at `ADAPTIVE_HISTORICAL_VALIDATION`.

The citations themselves are treated as leads. Nothing here rests on one, and
the 2026 SSRN item was not verified.

## A defect I created and then caught

`test_an_unpriceable_book_is_422_not_a_confident_full_exposure` started failing
with a **200**. The cause was mine: `backend/cache.py` keeps a diskcache at
`<repo>/.cache` shared by the app and the suite, and verifying the endpoint by
hand earlier had written a real 200 into it. The test patches the price fetch to
raise — and never reached it, because the cache answered first.

**The refusal test had been passing only because nobody had exercised the
endpoint.** That is the same family as this morning's wall-clock fixture: a test
whose verdict depends on a circumstance outside the code reports a defect on the
circumstance. Worse here, because exercising the thing you just built is exactly
what we tell ourselves to do.

Fixed in `conftest.py` (session-scoped redirect to a tmp dir; production path
untouched), and `test_suite_isolation.py` now asserts each redirection is *in
effect* — the conftest fixtures are all written `try/except: yield`, so a
silently-inert one is §47 waiting to happen.
