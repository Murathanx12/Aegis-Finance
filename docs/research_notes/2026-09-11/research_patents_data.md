## PART 2 — DATA ACQUISITION

### (a) fundamentals PIT free
- SEC EDGAR XBRL companyfacts: GET https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK{10-digit-padded}.json -> ALL us-gaap+dei facts ever filed by that company, each datapoint carries `filed` (true PIT date), `end`/`start`, `fy`, `fp`, `form`, `accn`. No key required, just a compliant `User-Agent: AppName contact@email` header. Rate limit 10 req/s per IP (use 9 to be safe), 429 on breach, sustained abuse can get a TCP reset/temp IP block.
- SEC EDGAR XBRL frames: GET https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/frames/us-gaap/{Concept}/USD/CY2024Q4I.json (instant) or CY2024 (annual)/CY2024Q1 (quarterly, +/-30d window) -> one fact per company for that period, good for building a cross-sectional panel of one metric at a time without 3,000 per-company calls.
- Bulk downloads (no rate limit, one big file, best for the ~3,000-name universe): https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/daily-index/xbrl/companyfacts.zip (all companyfacts), https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/daily-index/bulkdata/submissions.zip (all filing histories), quarterly Financial Statement Data Sets at https://www.sec.gov/dera/data/financial-statement-data-sets. This is the practical path for 3,000 names — pull the zip nightly/weekly instead of 3,000 rate-limited calls.
- Python libs: `edgartools` (pip install edgartools, MIT, github.com/dgunning/edgartools) — typed Company/Filing objects, built-in rate limiter (default 9 req/s via EDGAR_RATE_LIMIT_PER_SEC env var), local disk cache under ~/.edgar/, covers 10-K/10-Q/8-K/13F/Form 4/3/5/proxy/N-PORT/N-CSR etc, free, self-hosted, no key beyond `set_identity(email)`. `sec-edgar-downloader` (older, simpler, just bulk-downloads raw filing documents by form type/date range, MIT). Prefer edgartools for anything structured.
- EDGAR full-text search API: https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22...%22&forms=8-K — free, searches filing TEXT since 2001, useful for keyword/event scanning (e.g. find all 8-Ks mentioning "restructuring").
- Storage: parquet partitioned by `cik=/concept=/` or simply `date=YYYY-MM-DD/cik=XXXXXXXXXX.parquet` mirroring the JSON, keep `filed` as the PIT column always (never `end`/`start` for causality — CLAUDE.md's own "date a receipt by its own stamp" rule applies directly here).
- Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) free tier: 250 calls/day, delayed fundamentals, decent international coverage (some HK/JP tickers) — use as a cross-check/backfill, not primary, given the tiny daily cap. EODHD free tier: EOD prices only (fundamentals require paid), ~20 calls/day free — not very useful for fundamentals; their $19.99/mo "All World" tier if fundamentals across Asia are needed and WRDS doesn't cover post-2024 Asia.

### (b) earnings calendars/transcripts
- SEC 8-K Item 2.02 ("Results of Operations and Financial Condition") is the free, PIT-clean source for the announcement itself — filter EDGAR full-text search or submissions API by `form=8-K` and grep the filing for item "2.02"; timestamp is the `filed`/acceptance datetime (SEC now stamps acceptance time, useful for before/after-close classification).
- Finnhub free tier: 60 API calls/minute (generous), earnings calendar endpoint `/calendar/earnings?from&to&symbol` gives EPS estimate/actual + revenue estimate/actual, but FREE tier only returns ~1 year of history and US-market-only; fine for forward-looking calendar pulls, not for building a long PIT history (use SEC 8-K dates for history instead). No credit card required for free key.
- Alpaca: no free dedicated earnings-calendar endpoint as of this research (Alpaca's news API covers Benzinga-sourced news; a dedicated corporate-actions/earnings-calendar product is on paid plans) — treat SEC 8-K + Finnhub free calendar as the two free legs, cross-check dates.
- Earnings call TRANSCRIPTS free: no fully free, ToS-clean bulk source found; Motley Fool/Seeking Alpha transcripts are behind paywalls and their ToS generally bars scraping/redistribution — avoid. `earningscall` PyPI library wraps a paid transcript API (has a limited free trial tier, not a sustainable free source). Practical free-and-legal path: the underlying EARNINGS CALL AUDIO is often furnished as an 8-K exhibit or linked from the company's IR page; SEC filings themselves (the 8-K narrative + any furnished slide deck exhibits) are the only guaranteed-free, ToS-clean text. For genuine transcript text at scale, budget for a modest paid tier (e.g. Finnhub's paid "transcripts" add-on, or a specialized cheap transcript vendor) rather than treating it as free.
- Storage: parquet `event=earnings/date=YYYY-MM-DD/ticker=XXXX.parquet` keyed by the calendar date, with a separate `filed_8k` timestamp column for the actual 8-K PIT stamp (calendar date and filing date differ; before/after-market timing matters for T+0 vs T+1 return attribution).

### (c) options data
- Alpaca options data: bundled with the existing Alpaca account. Basic (free) plan = indicative pricing feed only (not real OPRA), historical option data limited to "latest 15 minutes" restriction (i.e., can't pull deep history on the free tier), 200 calls/min. Algo Trader Plus ($99/mo) unlocks full OPRA feed + unrestricted historical option data + 10,000 calls/min — worth it ONLY once options actually enter a live strategy; until then the free Basic tier is enough for daily EOD snapshots.
- Tradier: sandbox account is free and gives real (delayed) options chains + greeks for development; a funded Tradier brokerage account gets free real-time/historical market data API access bundled with NO extra data fee — cheapest path to real, continuously-updated options chains if opening a small funded Tradier account is acceptable alongside the existing Alpaca account.
- CBOE: two tiers — (1) free, unauthenticated delayed-quote JSON at `cdn.cboe.com/api/global/delayed_quotes/options/_{SYMBOL}.json` (backs the public cboe.com quote pages; undocumented/unsupported but currently live, good for daily EOD snapshots of index/major-name chains at zero cost); (2) CBOE DataShop / LiveVol All Access API — paid, points-based, 14-day free trial at 500 points/day, real historical tick-level options data back further than most competitors — reserve for later if the free CDN snapshot proves insufficient.
- yfinance `.option_chain()`: free, unofficial (scrapes Yahoo), gives a live snapshot of bid/ask/IV/OI per expiry — no history, so the ONLY way to build an options history from it is to snapshot it yourself daily and accumulate; fragile (Yahoo can break it without notice) but zero-cost and already familiar in this ecosystem.
- Given "2025-26, snapshot daily since history isn't free" in the ask: the practical free stack is Alpaca Basic (indicative) + a daily CBOE CDN snapshot + a daily yfinance chain snapshot for the ~3,000-name universe's LIQUID names only (options aren't listed on most small caps anyway) — store as parquet `options_snapshot/date=YYYY-MM-DD/underlying=XXXX.parquet`, one row per contract, and treat "history" as whatever you've accumulated since the day you started snapshotting (no free vendor sells pre-2025 daily options chains for the whole universe).
- Earnings-day implied move: computable for free from any of the above same-day chain snapshots (ATM straddle price / underlying price), no separate paid feed needed — this is one of the cheapest, highest-value derived series to start logging daily now, since it compounds only from the day you start.

### (e) alt data free
- Wikipedia pageviews: the OFFICIAL free source is the Wikimedia REST API `https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/metrics/pageviews/per-article/{project}/{access}/{agent}/{article}/daily/{start}/{end}` — no key, generous rate limits, daily granularity back to 2015. (Search results surfaced several third-party PAID wrapper services like "trendsapi.ai"/"trendsmcp.ai" rebranding pageviews/Trends — these are commercial resellers, not needed; use the official Wikimedia endpoint directly and skip them.)
- Google Trends: `pytrends` (unofficial scraper) is reported ARCHIVED/unreliable as of this research (frequent 429s, breaks on Google changes) — treat Google Trends as fragile/best-effort only, budget for occasional breakage, and note Google's ToS technically prohibits automated scraping (a real, if rarely enforced, risk).
- GDELT (official, gdeltproject.org): 100% free, bulk CSV/parquet event+GKG files back to 1979 at data.gdeltproject.org, updated every 15 min; also queryable via Google BigQuery's public dataset (free tier covers moderate usage). This is the OFFICIAL free path — ignore third-party "GDELT Cloud" paid wrapper APIs seen in search results (docs.gdeltcloud.com appears to be an unrelated/unofficial commercial service, not GDELT itself).
- USPTO bulk patent data: PatentsView migrated to the USPTO Open Data Portal (ODP, data.uspto.gov) as of March 2026; still free but now requires a USPTO.gov account login (mandatory from June 18 2026) plus extra profile fields from Aug 18 2026 — factor in a one-time signup step, not a pure anonymous API anymore.
- USAspending.gov API: https://api.usaspending.gov/api/v2/... — free, no key, contract/grant/award search + bulk CSV download endpoints; good for a "did this company just win a large federal contract" event signal.
- FDA PDUFA calendars: no single official free API found in this pass; practically sourced from FDA's own calendar pages / biotech-focused newsletters — budget manual/semi-automated curation for this one, it's a small enough universe (biotech names) to hand-maintain.
- Congress trading disclosures: PRIMARY free/official sources are the Senate EFD JSON API (efts.senate.gov, PTRs under the STOCK Act) and the House Clerk's year-to-date ZIP+PDF archive (disclosures-clerk.house.gov) — both public-domain, no key. Several free/open wrappers exist on top: `crnicholson/capitol-api` (GitHub, self-hostable, MIT-ish, parses both chambers into one REST API) and `quantengines.com` (hosted free tier, 20 req/min, no key). Quiver Quantitative's congress-trades API is the paid/cleanest commercial option if a hosted feed is preferred over self-hosting.
- Insider Form 4: already have 11.5M rows per this repo's own notes — no new source needed.
- Job postings: Greenhouse (`boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/{token}/jobs`), Lever (`api.lever.co/v0/postings/{company}`), Ashby (`api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/{company}`) all expose FREE, unauthenticated public JSON job-board APIs per company — no aggregator needed, but each requires knowing/discovering the company's board token, so build a small mapping table for the ~3,000-name universe's tech/growth subset rather than expecting universal coverage (many companies don't use these three ATS platforms or don't expose a public board).

### (f) Asian market data
- AKShare (pip install akshare, MIT license, github.com/akfamily/akshare): the single best free Python library for this — covers China A-shares (`stock_zh_a_hist`), Hong Kong, futures, options, bonds, macro, and more via one consistent interface; explicitly built for academic/research use (its own docs flag commercial-use caution). Also ships an offline interface registry (`ak.search(...)`) so an LLM-driven pipeline can resolve "what's the HK daily bars function" without network calls.
- Japan (TSE/JPX) and Korea (KRX): no equivalently good free official API found; practical free path is Yahoo Finance tickers via `yfinance` (`.T` suffix for Tokyo, `.KS`/`.KQ` suffix for Korea) or Stooq (`stooq.com`, free daily CSV, decent international coverage) — both unofficial/scraped, so treat as delayed/best-effort, not exchange-grade.
- HKEX: has some free delayed data on its own site; for anything beyond delayed quotes AKShare's HK functions or yfinance `.HK` tickers are the practical free options.

### General patent facts
- US patent term = 20 years from the EARLIEST claimed non-provisional filing date (35 U.S.C. Sec 154(a)(2)), not grant date; a provisional filing does not start the clock by itself. Patent Term Adjustment (PTA) for USPTO prosecution delay can extend the nominal +20y figure, which is why exact expiry dates are sometimes a few months/years later than a naive filing+20 calculation.
- Free full-text sources: Google Patents (patents.google.com) — fastest, full text/PDF/family/citations/a derived legal-status label; USPTO Patent Public Search (ppubs.uspto.gov/pubwebapp) and USPTO Patent Center (patentcenter.uspto.gov) — authoritative US status, prosecution history, maintenance-fee payment record; WIPO PatentScope (patentscope.wipo.int) — PCT/international family; Espacenet (worldwide.espacenet.com) — EPO worldwide family + INPADOC legal-status. Cross-check anything load-bearing on USPTO Patent Center since Google's status label can lag actual maintenance-fee lapses.
