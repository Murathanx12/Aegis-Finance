## Section 1: The problem, quantified

- Barber & Odean 2000 (J. Finance 55(2):773-806), 66,465 households 1991-96: avg household 16.4%/yr vs mkt 17.9%; top quintile by turnover (turnover 250%/yr, ~75% avg) earned 11.4% net -> ~6.5pp/yr gap from overtrading. https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/individual_investor_performance_final.pdf https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00226
- Barber & Odean 2008 "All That Glitters" (RFS 21(2):785-818): individuals net buyers of attention-grabbing stocks (news, high volume, extreme 1-day return) -> attention-driven buying, not selling (can only sell what you own). https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/allthatglitters_rfs_2008.pdf
- DALBAR QAIB 2026 (covers 2025): S&P500 17.88% vs avg equity investor 17.16% = 72bp gap (3rd smallest since 1985); 2024: S&P500 25.02% vs avg equity investor 16.54% = 848bp gap (2nd largest in decade). Fixed income gap 2025: Bloomberg Agg 7.30% vs avg FI investor 2.41% = 489bp. https://www.dalbar.com/press-release/dalbars-2026-qaib-report-shows-narrower-investor-gap-amid-a-complex-and-volatile-market-year/ https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/dalbars-2026-qaib-report-shows-143800294.html
  - CAVEAT: DALBAR's methodology is contested (dollar-weighted vs time-weighted framing overstates "bad timing" narrative) — cite critique below.
- Morningstar Mind the Gap 2025 (2015-2024): buy-hold return 8.2%/yr vs investor (dollar-weighted) actual 7.0%/yr = -1.2pp/yr gap; ~$3.8T aggregate shortfall over $13.6T asset base. Gap worse for volatile funds (-2.1%/yr) vs steady funds (-0.4%/yr). Bitcoin ETFs gap ~14pp/yr (investors -5.8% vs fund +8.5% through June 2026). https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/research/mind-the-gap
- CRITIQUE of "Mind the Gap"/DALBAR-style framing: Fulkerson, Jordan, Riley, Yan 2026 FAJ "Bad Timing Does Not Cost Investors 15% of Their Funds' Returns" — argues dollar-weighted vs time-weighted gap is mechanically inflated by fund flows/growth, not proof of bad market timing. https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2026/bad-timing-does-not-cost-investors-funds-returns https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4904652
- Disposition effect / home bias / under-diversification / lottery-stock pref / trend chasing / overtrading / trade clustering identified as the 8 key costly retail bias factors (Amundi research center review; Springer FMPM review). https://research-center.amundi.com/article/behavioural-biases-among-retail-and-institutional-investors https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-024-00444-7

- Kumar 2009 "Who Gambles in the Stock Market?" (J. Finance 64:1889-1933): lottery-type stocks preferred by low-income/unemployment-area investors; these stocks underperform by ~2-3%/yr for those who chase them. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01483.x
- Robo methodology: Wealthfront Classic Portfolio white paper (MPT-based, risk-tolerance-tuned, tax-efficient rebalancing, daily automated TLH; TLH designed to exceed the 0.25% advisory fee) https://research.wealthfront.com/whitepapers/investment-methodology/ https://research.wealthfront.com/whitepapers/tax-loss-harvesting/ ; Betterment TLH+ uses Monte Carlo-optimized harvest triggers https://www.betterment.com/resources/tax-loss-harvesting-methodology
- Robo-advisors do NOT stock-pick or read news — they allocate across low-cost ETFs by risk questionnaire + MPT/glide path, rebalance on bands/calendar, harvest losses; no company-specific or event-driven decisions.
- Academic perf: German robo study 2017-2022 (16 robos, 86 portfolios) benchmarked vs simple passive mixes — ResearchGate/diva-portal thesis. Robo AUM projected $297B(2017) -> $2.76T(2023E) -> $4.66T(2027E); users 7.49M(2021)->10.31M(2022). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336237020_The_Performance_and_Asset_Allocation_of_German_Robo-Advisors https://www.statista.com/statistics/755599/performance-of-selected-robo-advisors/

## Section 2: Agency shape / regulation
- SEC IM Guidance Update 2017-02 (Feb 23 2017): robo-advisers registered as RIAs under Advisers Act of 1940; guidance covers (1) disclosure of algo limitations/risks, (2) obtaining sufficient client info for suitability, (3) compliance program tailored to automated advice + accompanying Investor Bulletin. https://www.sec.gov/investment/im-guidance-2017-02.pdf https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2017-52
- Boundary (NOT legal advice, general pattern only): the Advisers Act defines "investment adviser" as one who advises OTHERS for compensation; someone managing only their own account, or a personal tool they alone run on their own account with no compensation/advice to third parties, generally falls outside the definition and registration trigger. This is a structural observation from the Act's definition, not a legal opinion — recommend the owner confirm with counsel before any multi-user or paid distribution.
- Investor.gov / IA registration overview: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/working-investment-professional/investment-advisers-0

## Section 4: social signals
- Cookson & Niessner (2020, "Why Don't We Agree?", Mgmt Sci): decomposes StockTwits disagreement into model-based (differing investment philosophy) vs info-based; their measure has marginal/no incremental predictive power over other disagreement measures once controlled. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/314379468_Why_Don't_We_Agree_Evidence_from_a_Social_Network_of_Investors
- Renault (2017, J. Banking & Finance 84:25-40): StockTwits-built sentiment lexicon; first-half-hour sentiment change predicts LAST half-hour S&P500 ETF return same day; effect driven by novice/noise traders — intraday only, decays same session. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3010856 https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v84y2017icp25-40.html
- GameStop/WSB 2021 literature (mixed): Reddit mention volume vs GME intraday retail volume rank-correlation 0.93; but positions opened when WSB attention peaked realized -8.5% holding-period returns on average — i.e. the crowd signal is a fade/late-entry trap more than an edge once attention is already extreme. https://www.nature.com/articles/s44260-025-00029-z (attention-timing effect) — note: numbers pulled from aggregated WebSearch synthesis, verify original papers before citing precisely (arXiv 2107.07361 "From Reddit to Wall Street").
- Cohen, Polk, Silli "Best Ideas" (2010, mutual fund managers' highest-conviction stock outperforms fund's other holdings + market by 2.8-4.5%/yr); but <19% of "best ideas" overlap across 2 managers, 8% across 3 — i.e. genuine best-idea signal is manager-specific and hard to crowd-source/copy. https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/24471/1/Best%20ideas(published).pdf https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1364827
- Owner's own prior finding (per project memory): 13F/insider/congress "copy-trading" signal already tested and found THIN — consistent with the 45-day 13F filing lag (institutions must file within 45 days of quarter-end, so retail copy-traders act on stale positions) and the low best-idea-overlap finding above — both point to the same mechanical reason copy-trading has weak edge.

## Section 5: books/theories
- Piotroski F-score (2000, J. Accounting Research, 1976-96 sample): long high-F-score/short low-F-score value stocks = 23%/yr; improved low P/B portfolio return by >=7.5pp/yr. Replication 2003-2015: high-F-score portfolio +18.3%/yr market-adjusted (holds up somewhat). BUT applying the exact original rule over the "last 10/20 years" (as of a 2020s critique) produced AVERAGE ANNUAL LOSSES of -9.53%/-11.75% long-short — strong decay/crowding once popularized. https://blog.portfolio123.com/why-piotroskis-f-score-no-longer-works/ https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotroski_F-score
- Greenblatt Magic Formula ("The Little Book That Beats the Market", 2005): backtested ~30%/yr 1988-2004. Reasonable Deviations & quant-investing critiques: pre-2007 ~26%/yr, but 2007-2010 drawdown -57% and went cumulatively negative; much of the edge concentrated in small caps; costs/slippage excluded in original; "excess cash" definition undisclosed, complicating replication. Verdict: works pre-costs in backtest, materially weaker/negative after realistic costs+small-cap liquidity+recent decades. https://reasonabledeviations.com/2020/06/08/greenblatt-magic-formula/ https://www.quant-investing.com/blog/magic-formula-investment-strategy-back-test
- Antonacci Dual Momentum / GEM ("Dual Momentum Investing", 2014/2015): backtest claimed near-equity return at ~half the drawdown. TRUE out-of-sample since publication: lagged plain 60/40, and the drawdown edge was largely a 2008-only story; one full-period (2010-2025) backtest: 7.9%/yr vs 8.8%/yr benchmark = underperformance post-publication. https://awealthofcommonsense.com/2015/07/my-thoughts-on-gary-antonaccis-dual-momentum/ https://quant4free.com/analysis/dual-momentum/
- Faber Ivy/GTAA ("A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation" 2007 + "The Ivy Portfolio"): 10-month SMA trend filter reduced vol/drawdown in backtest. REAL PRODUCT: the GTAA mutual fund/ETF launched on this research underperformed buy-and-hold badly and was closed May 2017 (2011: 5-fund GTAA model +2.49% vs the actual ETF -7.29%) — a clean "backtest vs product-after-costs-and-complexity" gap. https://canadiancouchpotato.com/2013/12/30/the-failed-promise-of-market-timing/ (product closure) 

- Graham margin of safety / net-net (Security Analysis / Intelligent Investor): buy below net current asset value. Graham's own 1957-59 test: +75% vs S&P +50%. Oppenheimer 1970-83: net-nets +29.4%/yr vs NYSE-Amex +11.5%/yr. Modern replications (Carlisle etc.) ~15-25%/yr backtested 1950s-2010s, but opportunity set now rare/small-cap/illiquid in efficient modern markets — the RULE still backtests well when it can find candidates, but candidates are scarce today. https://alphaarchitect.com/an-analysis-of-grahams-net-nets-outdated-or-outstanding/
- O'Shaughnessy "What Works on Wall Street" (1996, updated eds.): decades of factor backtests (value composite, growth+value combos). Post-2010 OOS check (Portfolio123): reversed — in the "Value Composite 2" cheap-stocks-outperform test since Jan 2010, the CHEAPEST decile performed WORST and the most expensive performed BEST, i.e. sign flip out of sample. General backtest-overfitting literature (Bailey et al., "Backtest overfitting in financial markets") explains the broader pattern. https://blog.portfolio123.com/did-what-works-on-wall-street-stop-working/ https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/overfit-tools-at.pdf
- Dalio All Weather / risk parity: in-sample/backtest era 1973-2010 excellent risk-adjusted returns; 2011-2021 "acceptable" but riding falling-rate tailwind; TRUE out-of-sample regime test came 2022 (rates spike): All Weather -22% in 2022 (bonds+equities both fell, diversification broke down); 2023 recovered +4.5% through Nov. Billions in outflows from major pension funds post-2022. https://www.thedailyupside.com/advisor/investing-strategies/is-ray-dalios-all-weather-etf-appropriate-for-a-long-summer/ https://www.benzinga.com/news/24/04/38371547/
- Bogle / Sharpe "Arithmetic of Active Management" (1991): mathematical identity — active dollar = passive dollar return before costs; active underperforms passive by costs, after fees, in aggregate. Bogle built indexing philosophy on this. 2019 Leippold & Rueegg: active funds beat passive BEFORE fees in aggregate but converge to ~zero net-of-fee edge — Sharpe's logic roughly holds empirically once costs included; finding an ex-ante identifiable outperforming manager after fees remains hard. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.2469/faj.v74.n1.4
