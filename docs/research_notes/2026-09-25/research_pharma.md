## Policy overlay: MFN drug pricing + Section 232 pharma tariffs (as of 2026-09-25)

**MFN pricing.** Trump's "Delivering Most-Favored-Nation Prescription Drug Pricing to
American Patients" executive order (2025-05-12) led to a sequence of voluntary
company-by-company deals: Pfizer first (2025-09-30), nine majors (2025-12-19) including
**Lilly, Novo, Amgen, Gilead, Merck**, Regeneron as the 17th deal (2026-04-23), AbbVie
also confirmed on the list, and nine more mid-sized manufacturers — Alcon, Astellas,
BeOne, **BridgeBio (BBIO)**, CSL, Kyowa Kirin, Sun Pharma, Teva, UCB — on 2026-08-31. As
of the 2026-09-18/19 White House releases, **26 companies covering ~89–90% of the branded
U.S. market** hold MFN deals (extended that week to every state Medicaid program),
committing ~$170bn to U.S. manufacturing/R&D and stocking a Strategic API Reserve. Named:
Pfizer, AstraZeneca, EMD Serono, Lilly, Novo, Amgen, BMS, Boehringer Ingelheim,
Genentech/Roche, Gilead, GSK, Merck, Novartis, Sanofi, J&J, AbbVie, Regeneron, Alcon,
Astellas, BeOne, BridgeBio, CSL, Kyowa Kirin, Sun Pharma, Teva, UCB. **Notably absent from
every list found: Vertex Pharmaceuticals and argenx** — neither has a confirmed MFN deal
as of today, a real (if second-order) tariff-exposure gap worth pricing into those two
names specifically. Sources: whitehouse.gov fact sheets (2026-04-23, 2026-08-31,
2026-09-18/19), Reuters 2026-08-31, CNBC 2026-08-31.

**Section 232 tariffs.** Proclamation 11020 (2026-04-02) imposed a **100% ad valorem
tariff on patented pharmaceuticals and APIs**, staggered effective 2026-07-31 (Annex III
companies) and **2026-09-29 for everyone else** — the broad go-live lands four days before
this report's catalyst window even opens. Companies with Commerce-approved **onshoring
agreements** pay 20% (stepping to 100% in 2030); companies combining onshoring **with** an
MFN deal pay **0% until 2029-01-20**. EU/Japan/Switzerland/South Korea/Liechtenstein
imports face a flat 15%; UK imports 0%. **Generics are exempt outright.** A 2026-09-23 BIS
notice (effective with the Sept 29 go-live) separately carves out **0%** for orphan drugs,
nuclear medicine, plasma-derived therapies, fertility drugs, cell/gene therapy, ADCs, and
CBRN countermeasures from qualifying jurisdictions or via a rolling "urgent U.S. health
need" petition (open since 2026-09-23). **Practical read for this book**: every large-cap
MFN signatory (all of Bucket 1 except VRTX and ARGX) is presumptively shielded at 0%; VRTX
and ARGX carry unresolved tariff exposure, partially offset for VRTX by mostly-domestic CF
manufacturing and for both by orphan/cell-gene/biologic carve-outs their pipelines may
independently qualify for. Sources: Federal Register 2026-04-02 (Proclamation 11020) and
2026-09-23 (BIS notice); KPMG TaxNewsFlash 2026-09-21; CFR "Guide to Trump's Section 232
Tariffs" 2026-09-17.

## Run-up vs. sell-the-news: PDUFA vs. Phase 3 readouts, and entry timing

**Rothenstein, Tomlinson, Tannock & Detsky (2011, *JNCI* 103(20):1507)**, oncology stocks
2000–2009: ahead of Phase III **trial readouts**, eventual-positive trials ran up **+13.7%**
over 120 days vs. **−0.7%** for eventual-negative (p=.09), tightening to **+9.4% vs. −4.5%**
over 60 days (p=.03) — a real anticipatory drift consistent with informed positioning
ahead of the print. For **FDA regulatory decisions**, the same study found **no**
significant pre-announcement drift differentiating eventual approvals from eventual CRLs.
**Hwang (2013, *PLoS ONE*, PMID 23951273)**, a later, larger-cap sample (2011–2013), found
**no pre-announcement run-up at all** for either event type — a genuine tension with
Rothenstein, plausibly sample composition (large diversified biopharma vs. oncology-heavy
small/mid-caps) and post-PDUFA-VI timing. Hwang's announcement-day reaction was
asymmetric: **+0.8%** median CAR on positive news, fading to insignificance by day +1, vs.
**−2.0%** on negative news persisting through (−2,+2).

A third, more current dataset (BioRadar, 69 PDUFA events May 2025–Sept 2026, sourced by
the large-cap research pass) sharpens this: eventual-approval stocks ran up **+15.6%** in
the 30 days pre-decision but moved only **−0.1%** on decision day and **+0.9%** five days
out — most of the good news is priced in *before* the date. Eventual-CRL stocks, by
contrast, showed a **−14.7%** pre-decision drift (some leakage/skepticism) plus a further
**−13.7%** on decision day and **−33.1%** over five days — rejections are 2–4x larger in
magnitude than approvals and are not cushioned by a positive run-up the way approvals are.

**Implication for entry timing in this book**: for **Phase 3 topline** catalysts
(especially oncology/small-cap), a meaningful share of the positive-scenario return has
historically already run by the 60–120 days before the print — size before that window
opens if conviction is high, or accept the edge is partly gone by the print date. For
**PDUFA-date** catalysts, the risk-adjusted trade favors being positioned *before* the
run-up (i.e., now, for names with a catalyst in Q4-2026/Q1-2027) rather than adding fresh
capital right at the date — approval upside is capped by the pre-date drift while CRL
downside is not. Note the literature's samples are large/mid-cap; small/micro-cap binary
moves in this book's Bucket 2 are plausibly larger in both directions than either study
captures — a real gap, sized for via lower per-name weight on the smallest names below.
