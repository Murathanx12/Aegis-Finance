# The expectation layer works, and 87–93% of what it finds was never tradable

**2026-08-16.** Two results, and the second one matters more than the first.

---

## 0. The correction that prompted this

G4 V1 reported:

> positive EPS (51,384 events) → **+0.25%** · beat by >1σ (28,010) → **+1.88%**
> — "seven times the signal from knowing what was expected"

Two reviewers caught the same defect independently and they are right. **Those
are overlapping sets and the second is a strictly tighter selection.** Any
tighter conditioning on a variable correlated with the outcome produces a larger
conditional mean. It was nested-set arithmetic wearing a finding's clothes.

The test that breaks the nesting is the factorial, and every cell was already in
the 60,402 records.

## 1. The 2×2 — the expectation does the work

Date-demeaned (each announcement date's common move removed exactly), 58,066
events across **3,129 announcement dates**, which is the inference unit. SEs from
a block bootstrap over dates: firms reporting on one day share that day, and
treating 58,066 rows as 58,066 observations is the §58 error.

```
                    surprise > 0     surprise <= 0
   EPS > 0          A  +1.33%        B  -2.27%
   EPS <= 0         C  +1.38%        D  -2.86%
```

**Does the expectation add anything, holding the announcement fixed?**

| contrast | | |
|---|---|---|
| A − B, surprise sign within positive EPS | +3.59pp | z +55.6 |
| **C − D, surprise sign within negative EPS** | **+4.24pp** | **z +16.7** |
| C − B, *lost money but beat* vs *made money but missed* | +3.64pp | z +17.5 |

**The mirror — does the announcement add anything, holding the expectation fixed?**

| contrast | | |
|---|---|---|
| A − C, EPS sign within a beat | −0.05pp | **below MDE, not detectable** |
| B − D, EPS sign within a miss | +0.59pp | z +3.69, resolvable |

**A company that lost money and beat expectations is indistinguishable from a
profitable company that beat.** The expectation is carrying the information; the
raw announcement adds nothing detectable on the upside and roughly a sixth as
much as the surprise on the downside.

That answers the question the layer was built to answer. Without it the factory
would be shown the EPS sign and learn the wrong variable.

## 2. And then the measurement was not honest enough

CRSP's daily return is **close-to-close**. For an after-hours report — which is
most of them — it contains the overnight gap: a move that happened while the
market was shut and nobody could act. Every number in §1 contains it.

The record now carries the split. `overnight_gap` and
`market_reaction_tradable` (open-to-close on the first tradable session, 99%
coverage from `crsp.dsf.openprc`), with the gap backed out of CRSP's **own
adjusted return** — `(1+ret) = (1+gap)(1+tradable)` — so splits and
distributions are handled by the vendor that knows about them rather than by
arithmetic on raw prices.

Both panels below are date-demeaned, so the **only** difference between them is
the gap:

```
  contrast   close-to-close   open-to-close   lost in the GAP
  A - B               3.59pp           0.26pp               93%
  C - D               4.24pp           0.55pp               87%
  C - B               3.64pp           0.18pp               95%
  A - C              -0.05pp           0.08pp    base below MDE
  B - D               0.59pp           0.37pp               38%
```

**87–95% of the announcement effect happens before anyone can trade it.**

What survives the open is +0.26pp (within positive EPS) and +0.55pp (within
negative EPS). Both are statistically resolvable — the sample is large enough to
see them — and both are **before costs**, on a strategy that would trade every
earnings event in the market.

`C − B`, the dramatic one, falls to +0.18pp and drops **below its own MDE**. The
"lost money but beat beats made money but missed" statement is a fact about the
overnight gap, not about anything anyone could have done.

## 3. What that settles

**The expectation layer is validated.** It carries information the announcement
does not, decisively, on the non-nested test. G4 keeps its priority over the
factory and the factory must be handed the surprise, not the EPS sign.

**Unconditional earnings drift is not a strategy on this data.** This is the
literature's answer (Martineau; the modern decomposition studies) reproduced on
our own panel rather than cited: the effect is real, large, and overwhelmingly
inside a window nobody can trade. Consistent with the order — **do not build
PEAD; build conditional earnings-underreaction discovery** — and the strategy
library seeds PEAD as a conditional question rather than a strategy.

**A ratio to a base below its own MDE is refused.** The `A − C` row would
otherwise have printed "253% lost in the gap" from dividing by noise, which is
the house error with a percent sign on it.

## 4. What this is NOT

* Not a pre-registered trial. No verdict is recorded, no slice is claimed, no
  confirmation budget is spent. G4 is a data layer and this is its plumbing
  test.
* Not a cost model. The surviving 0.26–0.55pp is gross, and the cost literature
  finds drift concentrated in exactly the names where costs are highest.
* Not a drift measurement. Everything here is the **first session**. Whether
  anything remains over 5/20/60 days is a separate question with a separate
  power calculation, and it belongs in a registration with a reserved window.
* Not evidence about 2020–2026. Every number is from 2006–2019, which is the
  declared selection period for this family. The confirmation window is reserved
  and now has a declared budget.
