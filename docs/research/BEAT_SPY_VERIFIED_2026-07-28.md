# What has actually beaten the S&P 500 — verified evidence review (2026-07-28)

Own research (5 parallel verification streams + 3 spot-checks). **Every number
below was read off a fetched primary document**; items computed locally are marked
[OWN CALC]; unverifiable items are quarantined in §6 and must not be printed.
**No fabricated papers were found** — every named paper exists, which is itself a

Status: **evidence review, not registration.** Nothing here graduates. Adverse

## 1. The single most actionable finding: INTEGRATE, do not STACK

Novy-Marx, *Quality Investing*: a **single integrated sort on B/P AND gross
profitability** in the 500 largest non-financials returns **7.4%/yr**, versus
3.2% for GP alone, 3.8% for B/P alone, and **3.5% for the two run side-by-side as
separate sleeves** — net of Hasbrouck costs, annual rebalancing.

His Table 7 trap is sharper still: running quality *beside* value raises the
information ratio but **lowers the Sharpe ratio** — every 50/50 mix underperformed
value alone (SR 0.47–0.52 vs 0.54), and "in every case except gross profitability
the long-only ex-post mean-variance efficient portfolio is fully invested in
value."

**This resolves the "combine ideas" question with a real answer.** Combination is
not free (Novy-Marx's multiple-signals result) *and* the sleeve-based architecture
is the wrong way to do it anyway. The right form is one integrated sort, which is
also a single registration rather than a combinatorial one.

**Candidate #4 (stacked combination) is DEAD.** Replaced by an integrated
value×profitability sort.

## 2. Candidate #1 (unlevered Buffett) — half dead

**The BAB half is dead for this house.** Novy-Marx & Velikov (JFE 2022,
verbatim-confirmed): *"For each dollar invested in BAB, the strategy commits on
average $1.05 to stocks in the bottom 1% of total market capitalization"*;
rank-weighting is *"a backdoor to equal-weighting."* Value-weighted BAB Sharpe
**0.49 vs 1.08**; FF5 alpha **24 bp (t=1.63), insignificant**; net of costs
**16 bp (t=1.20)**. Independently, Liu-Stambaugh-Yuan (JFE 2018) show the beta
anomaly is IVOL in disguise, and Bali et al. (JFQA 2017) show it vanishes once
neutralised to lottery demand. BAB is a **levered microcap trade we cannot run.**

**The quality half survives, small.** Novy-Marx JFE 2013 long-only gross
profitability: **CAPM α 1.44%/yr — the only one of seven quality definitions
significant at 5%** — turning over roughly once every four years. That fits the
turnover law exactly, and it is honest at ~1.4%/yr, not factor-headline size.

**Conflict flag:** AQR authored BAB, QMJ, Buffett's Alpha, trend and
size-matters-if-you-control-your-junk — and sells all of them. The unconflicted
refutations (Hou-Xue-Zhang, Huang et al., Novy-Marx & Velikov, Liu-Stambaugh-Yuan,
Gonçalves & Leonard) are the ones to weight.

## 4. Adverse findings against LIVE trials — recorded, not acted on

Both of these are **prior-setting and interpretation caveats**. Neither touches a
frozen forward trial; a live trial's registered decision rule is the only thing
that decides it.

- **TSMOM-XA.** Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (JFE 2020) is the strongest refutation
  found in this review: **47 of 55 assets have |t| < 1.65**; the pooled t=4.34 is
  *below* both bootstrap 5% critical values; and a strategy requiring **zero
  predictability** performs virtually identically. Bhardwaj-Gorton-Rouwenhorst
  (NBER w14424): CTA **net excess returns averaged 85 bp/yr, insignificantly
  different from zero**, 1994-2007. **This is consistent with our own disclosure**
  — TSMOM-XA was registered as DEFENSIVE with a declared return drag (t −1.86),
  explicitly *not* a beat-SPY claim. The external evidence strengthens that
  framing rather than contradicting it. No action; the maxDD decision rule stands.
- **TRIAL-CMP-INSIDER-IC.** Oenschläger & Möllenhoff (FRL 2025, 72:106514):
  insider-following returns *"vanish and even become negative when limiting the
  tradable dollar amount to a reasonable size"* — **before** transaction costs.
  Positive percentage returns, negative dollar returns. Cohen-Malloy-Pomorski's
  82 bp/mo is **GROSS**. Recorded as a caveat on the forward IC clock: a positive
  IC would not by itself imply a tradable edge.

## 5. What does not work (base rates, for the stopping rule)

- **SPIVA Mid-Year 2025:** **85.98%** of US large-cap funds underperformed the
  S&P 500 over 10 years, **91.03%** over 20. Risk-adjusted: **94.39% / 96.17%**.
  Only **34.70%** of the 20-year cohort survived.
- **Trading more:** Barber & Odean (JF 2000) highest-turnover households
  **11.4%/yr vs 17.9%** market. Morningstar *Mind the Gap* 2025: **−1.2pp/yr**,
  widening monotonically with trading activity.
- **Concentration, mechanically:** Bessembinder (JFE 2018) — *"the best-performing
  4% of listed companies explain the net gain for the entire US stock market since
  1926."* The modal concentrated portfolio underperforms even when the mean is
  fine. **This wounds candidate #5** (20-40 name concentrated book): the
  distribution is the problem, not the holding period.
- **Macro / market timing:** Goyal, Welch & Zafirov (RFS 2024) — of 29 post-2008
  predictors plus the original 17, *"more than one-third no longer have empirical
  significance even in-sample. Of those that do, half have poor out-of-sample
  performance."*
- **LLM / news alpha — a NEW receipt for NEG_RESULTS §19:** Gao, Jiang & Yan
  (arXiv:2512.23847) find LLM predictive power *"is amplified on high-LAP
  [memorized] firm-date pairs, and the interaction loses significance on
  post-training-cutoff samples."* Even the optimistic Lopez-Lira & Tang concede
  returns decline as LLM adoption rises and that the 90% hit rate is on the
  **explicitly non-tradable** initial reaction.
- **Index-effect trading:** dead. Greenwood & Sammon (JF 2025): **7.4% in the
  1990s → <1%**; deletions 0.1% (2010-2020).

## 8. The meta-finding

Three doors remain open, and none of them is "more signals":
1. **Construction** — integrate sorts rather than stacking sleeves.
2. **Turnover discipline as a design constraint, not a post-hoc check** — Novy-Marx
   & Velikov: strategies below ~50% one-sided monthly turnover keep significant net
   spreads; few above it do.
3. **A different data axis** — EDGAR disclosure-based copying is the only family
   with a published *net-of-cost* success.

Our 158 rejections are consistent with this literature. Combined with
Harvey-Liu-Zhu's **t > 3.0** hurdle and McLean-Pontiff's **58%** decay, further
cross-sectional mining has near-zero expected yield. That door is correctly closed.
