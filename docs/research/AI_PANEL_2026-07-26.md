|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1999–2009 CAGR | +0.3% | **−3.2%** (maxDD −82%) | +2.1% | −0.7% (−83%) |
| 2010–2019 CAGR | 13.3% | 16.6% | 14.4% | 17.7% |
| 2020–2026 CAGR | 15.0% | 23.4% | 9.1% | 19.9% |
| **Full 1999–2026 CAGR** | **8.5%** | 9.9% (maxDD −82%) | **8.2%** | 10.6% (−83%) |

- **XLV beats SPY in 74% of rolling 10-year windows yet LOSES over the full
  period** (8.2% vs 8.5%). "Beats many years" and "beats over the whole
  ride" are different claims; win-rate statistics flatter sector funds while
  terminal wealth doesn't.
- XLK's edge is one regime (2010+) purchased with an −82% drawdown in the
  prior decade. Rolling-window "beat rates" (80% for XLK) are dominated by
  windows ending in the tech regime.
- Mechanism: those bank funds hold concentrated sector beta. When the
  sector wins the decade, the funds "beat the market" — no stock-picking
  skill required (SPIVA: ~85–90% of active large-cap funds underperform the
  S&P over 15y; the advertised winners are the survivors). Replication is
  trivial — hold XLK — but it is a regime bet with fatter drawdowns, not
  alpha, and it is exactly the bet that destroyed the 1999 cohort.
- House translation: this is ALLOCATOR-class exposure choice, not evidence
  against the wall. If we want the bet, the honest instrument is a
  pre-registered sector-rotation/regime allocator (JM2 family), never a
  claim that sector funds found stock-level edge.
