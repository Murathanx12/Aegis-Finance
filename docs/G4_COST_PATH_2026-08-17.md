# The surviving edge lives exactly where it cannot be traded

**2026-08-17.** The third thing owed on the G4 result: the 0.26pp/0.55pp that
survived the overnight gap had no cost path. It has one now, and the prediction
written down before the numbers existed was right in both halves.

---

## The prediction, recorded before the measurement

> "largest in the illiquid tercile, dies there under any reasonable cost bound"

Put in writing so it could be embarrassed. It was not.

## The measurement

`C − D` — surprise sign within negative EPS, the non-nested contrast — on the
**open-to-close** return only, date-demeaned, split into dollar-volume terciles
**within each year** (volume grows roughly tenfold across 2006–2019, so a pooled
cut would put the early sample in "illiquid" and measure the calendar).

```
  tercile         n  median $vol       C-D      SE      z     MDE   break-even
  illiquid   19,214 $        11M     0.83pp   0.23pp   +3.5   0.66pp    20.7bp
  mid        19,204 $        49M     0.36pp   0.28pp   +1.3   0.79pp     9.1bp  [below MDE]
  liquid     19,211 $       179M    -0.20pp   0.36pp   -0.6   1.00pp    -5.1bp  [below MDE]
```

**Monotone in liquidity, and only detectable at one end.** illiquid − liquid =
+1.03pp. In the liquid tercile the point estimate is *negative* and nowhere near
its own MDE.

That is Ng, Rusticus & Verdi reproduced on our panel rather than cited: the
drift lives where the costs are. It is not a coincidence and it is not
recoverable by trying harder — the same illiquidity that leaves the mispricing
un-arbitraged is what makes it unprofitable to arbitrage.

## Why the break-even is the headline and "net of costs" is not

A net return is my cost assumption plus the data. A **break-even** is the data
alone. "This dies above 20.7 basis points per crossing" is a fact; "net of my
assumed 25bp it loses 0.17pp" invites an argument about my spread model instead
of about the edge.

**Four crossings, not one.** `C − D` is long-short: in on both legs, out on both
legs. Break-even per crossing is `edge / 4`, and quoting the edge itself as the
survivable cost would overstate it fourfold.

So: **20.7 basis points per crossing, on stocks with $11M of daily volume,
including a short leg.** No borrow cost, no market impact, and no allowance for
the fact that a strategy trading every earnings event in the market moves the
prices it trades. Against an illustrative schedule of 25/10/4bp by tercile all
three die, but the schedule is an assumption and the break-even is not — a
reader who thinks 20bp is achievable on an $11M-ADV short can substitute their
own number and see it for themselves.

## What this closes

The G4 chain is now complete end to end and it terminates in a negative:

1. The expectation layer carries information the announcement does not
   (`A − C` below MDE — a loss-making beat is indistinguishable from a
   profitable beat). **Validated.**
2. 87–95% of that effect occurs while the market is shut. **Untradable.**
3. What survives the open is concentrated in the illiquid tercile and breaks
   even at ~20bp/crossing there. **Uneconomic.**

Each step was measured rather than assumed, and each one is a different reason
the same finding does not become money. This is the third verdict family the
programme has needed: not *real* and not *not established*, but **real,
observable beforehand, and unreachable because the execution boundary binds
rather than the information**.

The Micron test asks whether a precursor was observable before the fact. Here it
was, comfortably. The market was closed.

## What it does not close

* This is the **first session only**. Whether anything remains over 5/20/60 days
  is a separate question with a separate power calculation. The 20d/60d
  horizons dilute the gap's share, so this result does not transfer to the
  longer-horizon families — N9's included.
* The cost schedule is anchored on published taxonomies, not measured. We have
  no TAQ, and estimating an effective spread from daily bars would be inventing
  precision. `hl_range_20d` is carried as a ranking variable only: at a 2.3%
  median it is an upper bound so loose as to be uninformative about spreads.
* **Nothing here is registered.** No slice claimed, no confirmation budget
  spent, no verdict recorded. This is a data layer reporting its own limits on
  its selection window (2006–2019), which is declared EXPLORE.
