## 1 · What v2 is, and what it deliberately did not touch

v1 (`learner_v1.json`) stands **untouched and reproducible**. v2 recomputed v1's
champion book from v1's own sealed OOS predictions and got it back **byte for
byte** (`v1_reproduction_check: REPRODUCED`, 19/19 keys) — so the two receipts
are on one footing. `evaluate.book()`'s default output is unchanged; the v2
additions are opt-in flags, pinned by test.

Four things are new:

1. **A shared encoder with per-horizon heads** (`learner/encoder.py`) — one
   trunk, eight heads (expected excess *and* P(excess > 0) at 1/3/6/12 m),
   fitted once per test year instead of eight times, on **byte-identical
   features to v1's 49 columns**.
2. **Money at every horizon.** v1 graded 3/6/12 m on rank IC alone, so its most
   striking number — the prior's IC t rising to 34.5 at 12 m — had no price
   attached. `evaluate.overlapping_book` prices a monthly-formed, h-month-held
   book (a new top-50 cohort each month, portfolio = 1/h in each live cohort,
   weights set at formation and left to drift, a name whose return goes missing
   liquidated into cash).
3. **Calibration** (`learner/calibrate.py`) — v1 shipped a classifier champion
   and not one Brier point.
4. **Paired tests between arms**, and an **empirical null distribution** for
   every headline statistic.

**The one preprocessing change from v1:** the encoder clips standardised
features at ±5 sd. Without it the regression heads returned an sd of **15.3** in
excess-return units — a predicted +1,533 % — because `ratio` is unbounded and
the panel holds name-months with a ratio in the hundreds. So `encoder vs v1 MLP`
is architecture **plus** clip, not architecture alone. Stated, not buried.

### The leak a shared trunk invites, and how it is closed

v1's split rule is *"train on rows whose target had matured before the test year
opened"*. With four targets per row there are four maturity dates, and the naive
multi-task fit — admit a row if **any** target matured, then train every head on
it — hands the 12 m head eleven months of the test year. Closed structurally: a
row is admitted if any horizon matured, then **every horizon's target is
individually masked** unless its own `mat_date` is strictly before the cutoff. A
masked target contributes exactly zero gradient.
`test_learner_v2_pit.py` asserts the mask *and* asserts that the four masks
**disagree with each other** — because the buggy implementation also produces
four identical masks, and a guard that passes on the bug is not a guard.

## 2 · THE NULL: what went wrong, what caused it, and what it costs

This is the main result of the session and it is a correction to my own method.

### 2.1 What the pre-registration said, and what happened

The header declared: *"expected: IC ~ 0, |t| < 2. If the null scores, EVERY
number in this receipt is void and says so."* The encoder's shuffled-target null
came back at 1 m with **rank IC t = −3.11**, and at 3/6/12 m its *book* earned
**+8 to +11 %/yr** with paired t 1.3–1.8. By the declared rule, void.

That rule was wrong, and the diagnosis is measured rather than argued.

### 2.2 Three hypotheses, and which one survived

| hypothesis | prediction | measured |
|---|---|---|
| **Target leakage** | a consistent SIGN — leakage makes a model look good, not bad | **REFUTED.** Across 16 shuffle seeds × 2 arms × 2 heads the null's IC t ranges **−9.16 to +11.99** (sd **4.78** at 12 m, against a nominal 1) and flips sign between adjacent seeds (12 m: −2.92 → +3.19 → −1.22 → +3.74). Leakage does not alternate. |
| **Degenerate predictions** | near-constant output, book decided by the tie-break | **REFUTED.** Null predictions have real cross-sectional spread and the null books differ from `random_rank` in composition and in return. |
| **A mis-specified null STATISTIC** | \|t\| > 2 should be common in legitimate null draws | **CONFIRMED.** \|IC t\| > 2 occurs in **34 % (1 m), 44 % (3 m), 62 % (6 m), 61 % (12 m)** of the 64 null draws per horizon. The null statistic's own sd is ≈ 2–3, and it was being compared to a threshold of 2. |

**The mechanism.** A model fitted on permuted targets still emits a *smooth,
feature-based ranking*. That ranking is applied to **every month**, so the book
takes one persistent factor tilt and holds it for 107 months, and the monthly
ICs are strongly serially correlated. The naive across-months t assumes they are
independent. This is the *same* error as the overlap artefact in §4, one level
up: the n is not the number of months.

### 2.3 The control that could NOT have caught it

I first built the obvious null: **200 random rankings**, each a real top-50 VW
book on the same rows and costs.

| horizon | random-book excess/yr | sd | paired t max over 200 | P(t ≥ 2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 m | −3.58 % | 2.77 pp | **+1.72** | 0.000 |
| 3 m | −1.69 % | 1.68 pp | +1.41 | 0.000 |
| 6 m | −1.24 % | 1.21 pp | +1.84 | 0.000 |
| 12 m | −0.83 % | 1.00 pp | +2.19 | 0.005 |

Two things fall out. First, **a random top-50 VW book LOSES to the VW market**
by 1–4 %/yr — the honest zero for this construction is negative, not zero.
Second, and this is the point: 200 random rankings never reached t 2, while a
single shuffled-target *model* reached **t 2.48**. A random ranking
re-randomises every month, so its tilts wash out; a model fitted on noise does
not. **The right null for a model is a model fitted on noise, not a coin flip.**

### 2.4 The model-null distribution, and what the arms actually score against it

64 draws per horizon (16 seeds × 2 arms × 2 heads), same clean universe, same
costs:

| horizon | null paired t p50 / p95 / **max** | best arm | its t | **p** | its TW | null TW max | **p (TW)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 m | 0.26 / 1.82 / **2.56** | `encoder_clf__residual` | **2.64** | **0.015** | 18.28× | **19.69×** | **0.046** |
| 3 m | 0.46 / 1.79 / **2.61** | `encoder_clf__residual` | **3.42** | **0.015** | 21.43× | 17.91× | 0.015 |
| 6 m | 0.26 / 1.50 / **2.88** | `lgbm_clf` | **3.70** | **0.015** | 16.22× | 21.25× | 0.031 |
| 12 m | 0.11 / 1.52 / **1.79** | `encoder_clf__residual` | **2.65** | **0.015** | 8.89× | 8.04× | 0.015 |

(p is one-sided, `(#null ≥ arm) + 1)/(n+1)`; no null draw exceeded the best arm's
t at any horizon, so 0.015 is the floor this many draws can express.)

**And the second finding, which is about my own pre-registration.** The
*declared primary metric was terminal wealth*. It is the **weakest**
discriminator here: at 1 m three of 64 null draws compounded **higher** than the
champion, one of them to 19.69× against the champion's 18.28×. The paired t is
the statistic that separates. The reason is measurable — see §2.5.

### 2.5 Why terminal wealth cannot rank these books: breadth 6–11, not 50

Measured on the actual holdings:

| book | top weight | **effective names (1/ΣwΣ)** | holdings ratio ≥ 5 | holdings ratio ≥ 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| top-50 by market cap | 0.091 | 30.6 | — | — |
| `encoder_clf__residual` | 0.340 | **7.3** | 0.3 % | **0.04 %** |
| `lgbm_clf` | 0.282 | **9.8** | 0.1 % | **0.00 %** |
| `random_rank` | 0.374 | **6.3** | 13.0 % | 6.9 % |
| `NULL_shuffled__encoder_raw` | 0.286 | **11.4** | 26.3 % | **12.75 %** |

A "top-50 value-weighted" book on this universe is a **6–11 bet** portfolio.
[[feedback-count-bets-not-tickers]], again. Its terminal wealth is a fat-tailed
statistic: two *arbitrary* rulers in this same receipt — `constant` and
`random_rank` — differ by **3.82× vs 6.34×** on nothing but the tie-break.

The same table carries the second mechanism. `ratio = mean_target / close ≥ 50`
is a **stale analyst target across a stock split**, not an opinion
([[feedback-a-stale-target-across-a-split-is-not-an-opinion]]). The trained
models exclude those rows almost perfectly (**0.00–0.04 %** of holdings); the
shuffled nulls, having no reason to, hold **8–13 %**. Those rows have vol_20d
0.77 vs the champion's 0.34, and they are why the null books have violent tails.
Decomposing the 1 m null's excess by name-month: its **top 1 % of rows carry
411 %** of its total and its **top 10 rows carry 562 %** — the rest is negative.
The champion's top 1 % carry **61 %** and its top 10 rows **28 %**.

**The contamination control** (declared before running, applied identically to
every arm including the nulls): drop name-months with `ratio ≥ 50` — 6.01 % of
rows. It flattens the 1 m null's *money* (−0.11 % → −0.34 %/yr, t −0.04) and
does **not** fix its IC. Every number in §3 onward is on this clean universe.

### 2.6 Verdict on the null

**The non-flat null is a mis-specified null statistic, not leakage and not a
degenerate pipeline.** The v2 numbers are not void. What *is* void is the
pre-declared bar: `|IC t| < 2 on one draw` should never have been written for a
model-based null, and the receipt now carries an empirical null distribution
instead. The cost of the error was one bar, not one result — but it would have
been a false *green* just as easily as a false red, and it was pre-registered.

## 4 · The horizon question — and the correction I nearly published

A sibling result landed the same day
(`docs/TRIAL_RESULT_2026-09-03_BAND_HORIZON.md`,
`band_horizon_20260903.json`): the prior's famous **IC t 34.5 at 12 m** is an
**overlap artefact** — n_effective is ~8, not 96. I had built the 12 m head
partly *because* of that number. Republishing it one document later would have
been the same error twice, so `evaluate.overlap_corrected` was added and every
IC t in this receipt now carries `t_naive`, `t_newey_west` and `t_block` with
its n.

| arm | 1 m | 3 m | 6 m | 12 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `prior` t_naive | 12.71 | 20.22 | 28.19 | **34.52** |
| `prior` **t_block** | 12.71 | **13.45** | **13.66** | **12.34** |
| `encoder_clf__residual` t_naive | 8.46 | 12.88 | 15.19 | 18.79 |
| `encoder_clf__residual` **t_block** | 8.46 | **8.52** | **7.32** | **6.87** |
| n_effective (date blocks) | 107 | 35 | 17 | **8** |

Independently reproduced: the naive t climbs, the block t is **flat or
declining**. The IC *level* does rise with horizon (0.061 → 0.079 → 0.084 →
0.100 for the champion); the IC *evidence per independent observation* does not.

**What this does and does not touch.** The IC t is inflated by overlap. The
overlapping book's **terminal wealth and drawdown are exactly right** — those
monthly returns are earned sequentially by a real account and compound honestly.
What overlap does to the *book* is make its monthly returns serially correlated
(it turns over 1/h of itself per month), so the paired t needs a HAC correction,
which the receipt carries. Money is unaffected; evidence is.

**V2-H2 (money follows IC across horizons): SUPPORTED on the declared rule,
and the rule was too generous.** Best paired t by horizon: 2.64 (1 m) → 3.42
(3 m) → 3.70 (6 m) → 2.65 (12 m). It rises then falls; the honest reading is a
**mid-horizon plateau at 3–6 m**, not "longer is better". The 12 m head is where
n_effective collapses to 8 and I would not build a decision on it.

## 5 · Calibration: is `P(beat) = 0.494` a probability?

**The reference is not 0.5.** The realised base rate of a US common stock
beating the **value-weighted** market is **0.4577 (1 m)**, 0.4409 (3 m), 0.4213
(6 m), **0.3967 (12 m)**. Individual-stock excess returns are right-skewed, so
most names lose to a cap-weighted index most months, and a forecast below 0.5 is
the *normal* case carrying no bearish content.

| arm (1 m) | Brier | base-rate Brier | BSS | mean pred | **bias vs base rate** | ECE | slope | recal. gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lgbm_clf` (v1, the shadow's model) | 0.24697 | 0.24781 | +0.0034 | 0.4754 | **+2.22 pp** | 0.0222 | 1.141 | +0.06 % |
| `encoder_clf__raw` | 0.24697 | 0.24781 | +0.0034 | 0.4737 | +2.05 pp | 0.0205 | 0.805 | +0.07 % |
| **`encoder_clf__residual`** | 0.24672 | 0.24781 | **+0.0044** | 0.4694 | **+1.62 pp** | **0.0162** | 0.866 | **−0.02 %** |

### The answer to the shadow-book question

**`P(beat) = 0.494` does NOT mean "the model dislikes today".** Read against the
base rate of **0.4577**, and after removing the model's own uniform **+2.2 pp**
level bias, 0.494 debiases to **≈ 0.472** — about **+1.4 pp above the base
rate**, and roughly +0.55 sd above the model's own mean prediction (sd of
predictions is only **0.0344**). It is a mildly **positive** score. Reading it
against 0.5 is reading it against the wrong number.

**Is it a *literal* probability?** By the pre-declared rule (ECE ≤ 0.02 AND
slope ∈ [0.5, 1.5] AND recalibration gain < 1 %):

- **v1's `lgbm_clf`: NO — a ranking score with a decimal point.** It misses the
  bar by 0.2 pp of ECE. But the miss is *entirely a uniform level bias*: slope
  1.141, r² 0.972, every one of ten bins too high by +1.1 to +3.7 pp. Its
  **ordering is excellent and its level is 2.2 pp too optimistic.** Subtract the
  bias and it is a probability. Deciles run 0.373 → 0.514 realised — a 14 pp
  spread in P(beat), which is real ordering.
- **v2's `encoder_clf__residual`: YES, at 1 m and 3 m** — the first arm in this
  programme whose P(beat) is a literal frequency. Temporal Platt makes it
  *worse* (−0.02 %), which is what "already calibrated" looks like.

**Calibration decays with horizon, monotonically.** Bias +2.2 → +2.3 → +2.8 →
**+3.5 pp**; ECE 0.022 → 0.023 → 0.028 → **0.048**; reliability slope 1.14 →
0.89 → 0.79 → **0.58**. At 12 m `lgbm_clf`'s Brier skill score is **negative**
(−0.0011): it does not even order better than the base rate at that horizon.
Recalibration gain grows to 1.3–2.2 % at 12 m — so **if the 12 m head is ever
used, it must be recalibrated; the 1 m head must not be.**

Every calibrator here is fitted **only on months strictly before the month it
scores** (min 12 months of history; months without history are NaN and are
dropped from *both* sides of the comparison). A calibrator that knows 2024 while
scoring 2016 is the walk-forward lookahead, one level up.

## 7 · What did NOT improve, and what I will not claim

- **No new information.** v2 is a better model over v1's identical 49 features.
  The independent-selector count is **unchanged at zero new sources**. A shared
  trunk cannot manufacture information and did not.
- **The engine prior still loses money at every horizon** while carrying the
  highest IC in the receipt. v2 did not repair the incumbent; it routed around
  it.
- **Terminal wealth failed as the pre-declared primary metric.** Three of 64
  null draws beat the champion on it at 1 m. I would now pre-register the
  **paired t against a model-null distribution** as primary and quote terminal
  wealth as description.
- **The 1 m result is p ≈ 0.015 against 64 null draws, one horizon, one arm out
  of 17, selected after looking.** Under any multiplicity correction that is
  suggestive, not a `RESEARCH_CLAIM`. It stays `PRODUCT_EXPERIMENT`.
- **v1's MLP is still bad on money** (2.13× at 1 m, 0.80× at 12 m) despite IC
  t 8.68. Whatever the encoder fixed, it was not "neural nets work here".
- **Nothing was sealed, promoted, or given broker authority.** The shadow still
  runs on the incumbent.

## 9 · Queue, in the order I would do it

1. **Retire `|null t| < 2` as a bar, repo-wide.** Any receipt that nulls a
   *model* needs a null *distribution*: refit on permuted targets ≥ 16 times and
   quote an empirical p. This is cheap (80 s per draw here) and it is the only
   thing standing between this programme and a false green.
2. **Put the `ratio ≥ 50` contamination filter into `dataset.py`'s hygiene**
   (owner's call — it is read-only to me). 6 % of rows, and they are the tail
   that every fat-tailed result in this repo keeps landing on.
3. **Report effective breadth beside every book.** A "top-50" book with 7
   effective names should never again be described as 50 names.
4. **The 3–6 m plateau deserves the next experiment, not 12 m.** n_effective at
   12 m is 8.
5. **Do not use `lgbm_clf`'s probability level without the −2.2 pp debias**, and
   do not recalibrate the 1 m `encoder_clf__residual` head — it is already
   calibrated and Platt makes it worse.
